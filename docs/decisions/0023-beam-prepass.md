# 0023: A beam prepass starts each 8x8 tile's rays where they could first stop

Status: accepted, 2026-11-21

## Context

After decisions 0015 to 0021 the primary trace was still the largest GPU
pass on the floor machine (2019 MacBook Pro, i7-9750H, Radeon Pro 5300M,
Metal, `--mac-floor`, 1280x720 internal): about 9 ms at its sampled
minimum, 11 to 18 ms mean, of a 16.7 ms frame. Decision 0015 named a beam
or coarse depth prepass as the next step on the trace, and the engine plan
(rendering pipeline, step 2) allows a pass "per 8x8 tile with beam
optimization".

The sampled ray statistics showed where a ray's work went: 17 to 21 steps
per ray on the 8 m/s floor walk, half the rays sky at 11.5 to 16 steps each.
Every ray of a tile starts at the camera and climbs out of the same empty
cells as its neighbours before it reaches anything.

The constraint is exactness. The trace must give the hits the CPU reference
gives (`compare_hits`), and the trace is path dependent in one way: its
footprint test (`cell < t * pixel_angle * footprint_bias`) runs only where
descent restarts, at the ancestor-stack level. A ray restarted mid-way
descends from the root at a larger `t` and could stop at a coarse ancestor
where the ray from the camera did not. A prepass may not stop a ray short of
what it would request either: requests and brick stamps come only from the
cells a ray stops in or descends through.

## Decision

### The prepass (`traversal/beam.wesl`, `passes/primary/beam.wesl`)

One thread per 8x8 tile of camera rays (`BEAM_TILE`, generated by
`cargo xtask codegen` from `passes::primary::trace`), before the trace,
writes one start distance per tile into a new binding (9) of the trace's
layout, which both pipelines share.

- **The bundle.** The four corner pixels' directions bound the tile: a
  pinhole camera's ray directions are affine in the pixel position up to
  length, so every ray of the tile is a positive combination of the
  corners' and lies within the cone around their normalized sum `c`. Its
  half-angle is taken as a chord, `spread = max |d_corner - c|`, so at
  distance `t` every ray's point is within `spread * t` of `o + c t`. The
  march bounds that by a cube of half-width `r(t) = k1 t + k0`, with
  `k1 = spread * 1.0001 + 1e-5` and `k0 = 0.5 + 1e-5 |o|` voxels as
  rounding allowance. Both are linear in `t`, so each cube face moves
  linearly.
- **A step at `t`.** Choose the level `L >= 1` whose cells are at least as
  wide as the cube (grown by a quarter voxel) and as the footprint at `t`.
  The cube then overlaps at most two level-`L` cells per axis. Look each up
  from the root, sharing the descent while they share a cell. A cell either
  lies in an empty cell of some level `e >= L` (a clear mask bit), or is
  occupied: a set bit at level `L`, a uniform node, or a brick or
  not-resident node whose bit is set. The beam never reads leaf masks or
  bricks. One occupied cell ends the march at `t`.
- **The free box.** The empty cells hold a box `V` around the cube: on an
  axis the cube does not straddle, the overlap of every cell's extent; on
  an axis it straddles, the low cells' lower bound and the high cells' upper
  bound. Each corner region of `V` lies inside its own cell's empty cell,
  so `V` is empty down to every level from `L` on.
- **Advance** to the first `t` at which a cube face reaches a face of `V`
  (linear, so exact up to rounding inside `k0`). Never go past
  `4^(e_min + 1) / (pixel_angle * footprint_bias)`, beyond which the
  footprint test could stop a restarted ray at an ancestor of the smallest
  empty cell found. If a step makes no progress, or the iteration cap
  (`lod.beam_iterations` in `brick_cache.ron`, 64; 0 turns the prepass off)
  or the tree's edge is reached, the march ends at `t`.

### The restart (`cast_ray_from` in `traversal/tree.wesl`)

The trace reads its tile's distance `t_s` (camera rays only; rays read from
the hit buffer in tests never use it). The traversal starts in the state the
reference DDA reaches once every voxel boundary below `t_s` is crossed: per
axis `axis_voxel(v, od, dd, t_s, false)`, entered at the largest of those
boundary distances, through the highest axis on ties (the reference steps
tied axes lowest first). From there it descends from the root as usual.

Why the hit is unchanged:

- Every voxel the ray passes before `t_s` lies in some step's `V`. The
  restart voxel `g` touches the ray just before `t_s` within `k0`, so it
  lies inside one of those empty cells, at a level `e >= L`.
- Descending from the root at `g`, every node above `e` was found internal
  with its bit set by the beam's own lookup, so no uniform, brick or
  not-resident stop can fire there. The footprint test cannot fire at any of
  them either, by the cap on `t`. So `g` is in an empty cell, with no hit,
  request or brick stamp.
- After that the restarted ray takes only exact jumps (distances from
  integer boundaries, never accumulated). Every state it lands on before
  meeting the camera ray's path is in an empty cell. Where the two meet
  (the first non-empty cell either enters), both enter that cell at the
  same boundary crossing. A cell entered from outside is footprint-tested at
  entry by both, so the descent outcomes agree from there on.

The prepass and the trace record into the frame's encoder back to back. The
sampled timer (decision 0015) times the beam as its own pass, `beam` in the
`gpu passes` line, before the trace.

### Tests

`World_Render/tests/gpu_beam.rs` traces five cameras over a scene built to
catch a non-conservative beam, each at 316x180 (partial tiles on both
edges), without and with the prepass, and requires every hit record to be
bit for bit the same apart from the step count. It also requires the same
requested nodes and the same stamped bricks. The scene has pillars one voxel
wide 100 to 800 voxels away (a fraction of a pixel, falling between tile
corners), a one-voxel wall and a one-voxel plate seen edge-on, 200 lone
voxels in the air, a 32 m floor seen from 9 cm above at a grazing angle, and
a ridge not aligned to any cell. It runs at full resolution, footprint bias
1 and 6, and with every third brick evicted (1,652 requests). Request
priorities are not compared: a node's priority comes from whichever of its
rays wins the race to request it, which varies from run to run either way.
With the spread forced to 0 (a beam only as wide as the centre ray) all four
tests fail at the plate's edge. `gpu_trace`'s CPU comparisons, which use
rays from the hit buffer, are unchanged and pass, and so do the golden
files.

  within 1 to 2 ms of each other at the lower loads of the last three
  rounds). The walks also covered 617 to 973 m, depending on frame rate.

## Alternatives considered

- **A tighter beam, 4x4 tiles:** rays start closer (trace 5.2 against 5.7
  ms in the bench), but four times the prepass threads cost more than that
  (1.9 against 1.1 ms). 16x16 is cheaper to march but starts rays too far
  back. 8x8 measured best, as the plan guessed.
- **An ancestor stack for the beam,** like the trace's, so each step starts
  at the deepest node still holding its cells instead of at the root:
  implemented, and no measurable change in the bench (1.09 to 1.10 against
  1.11 ms at 64 steps). Not kept.
- **The classic center-ray beam** (march the tile's centre ray, stop
  descending where a cell is narrower than the beam, treat any non-empty
  coarse cell as a hit): it does not bound rays that pass beside the centre
  ray's cells. A non-empty cell next to the one the centre ray is in can
  hold a voxel an edge pixel hits first. Checking the up to eight cells the
  bundle's cube overlaps is the conservative form of the same idea.
- **Restart at the beam's distance as a point** (the voxel containing
  `o + d t_s`, entered at `t_s`): not a state the reference passes through
  when the point lies on or near a voxel boundary. The restart instead
  takes the reference's state after every boundary below `t_s`, which
  reuses `axis_voxel` and its tie rules.
- **A coarse depth buffer from the previous frame, reprojected:** cheaper
  per frame, but not conservative under motion and disocclusion without the
  same kind of verification, and it would add history state.
- **Starting rays at the beam distance minus a fixed back-off** instead of
  proving the restart: the footprint path dependence makes any fixed
  back-off either unsafe or useless.

## Consequences

- On the floor walk, steps per ray halve and the trace's sampled minimum
  falls from about 9 to about 6 ms. The prepass costs about 0.7 ms of GPU
  time back to back (bench), and 1.2 to 3.3 ms as sampled. The trace with
  its prepass is still the largest pass, and 60 fps on the floor machine
  still needs more (the next ideas below).
- The prepass is a latency-bound pass of 14,400 threads at 720p, which
  leaves most of the GPU idle while it runs. Overlapping it with other work
  (shade of the previous frame, the brick cache's apply pass) would hide it.
- `TimedPass` gains `beam` (six passes); the `gpu passes` line lists it
  first.
- The trace's layout gains binding 9 (the per-tile start distances), so
  every user of `trace_bind_group` passes a beam buffer. `TraceParams.limits.w`
  is the beam's iteration cap.
- `brick_cache.ron` gains `lod.beam_iterations` (64; 0 turns the prepass
  off, which is also how to A/B it in one binary).
- Exactness now also rests on the beam's argument above, guarded by
  `gpu_beam`. Any change to the trace's stop conditions (a new node kind, a
  different footprint test, a new side effect of passing through a cell)
  must be mirrored in `beam_lookup`'s occupied cases and in the `t` cap.
- Rays that would exhaust the iteration cap (4096) from the camera may now
  finish. None did on the walk or in the tests.
- Next ideas, in the order they look most promising: hide the prepass by
  overlapping it with independent GPU work; march on
  past the tree's edge (outside counts as empty) until the whole cube is
  outside, which proves every ray of the tile misses, and let such tiles
  skip the trace; refine where the beam
  stops (it stops up to one level-`L` cell short of a surface, which on the
  grazing floor is most of the remaining steps) with a second, narrower
  march over the last stretch, or per 4x4 quarter of a tile; reuse the beam
  for shadow rays (one bundle per tile towards the sun); and a cheaper beam
  step (with descents shared, the eight-cell loop is most of its time).
